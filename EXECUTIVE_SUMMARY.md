# Executive Summary: Retail Causal Impact Analysis

All numbers below come from `make all` on the default profile (10,000 synthetic customers, seed 42) and are reproduced in `reports/tables/`.

## What has been built

This repository answers three questions about a retail coupon campaign from observational data: did the coupons work, for whom, and can the answer be trusted. The campaign targeted loyal, recent, high-value customers, who spend more anyway, so a plain treated-versus-control comparison credits the coupon for spend that would have happened regardless. On the synthetic campaign the naive comparison reports a lift of **$23.61** per customer while the true effect is **$15.56**, an overstatement of about **50%**. Propensity weighting and doubly-robust estimation (IPW, AIPW) bring the estimate to **$15.71 to $15.99** with 95% intervals that contain the truth, and the weighting reduces covariate imbalance from a mean standardized mean difference of 0.100 to 0.011 with every one of the 20 covariates under the 0.1 threshold. Three heterogeneous-effect models (DRLearner, Causal Forest, X-Learner) are fit on a training split and evaluated on 3,000 held-out customers, where the Causal Forest's predictions correlate 0.84 with the true per-customer effect. The targeting comparison is the headline result: given the same 20% budget, ranking customers by predicted uplift yields **$23.19** of incremental spend per targeted customer, random targeting yields $15.41, and a conventional predictive model that ranks by expected spend yields **$13.83**, worse than random, because it selects loyal customers who would have bought anyway. Those policy numbers use doubly-robust pseudo-outcomes, which reproduce the true values to within about a dollar and therefore transfer to real data where the truth is unknown.

The estimate is stress-tested rather than asserted. An explicit causal graph lets DoWhy derive the adjustment set (loyalty, recency, frequency, monetary value, segment), and six refutation checks all pass: DoWhy's placebo treatment, random common cause and data-subset refuters, plus in-house checks on the headline AIPW estimate (a permuted-treatment placebo of $0.03 ± $0.23, an IPW "effect" on a pre-campaign variable of $0.01 with an interval covering zero, and leave-one-covariate-out stability). The Cinelli and Hazlett robustness value is 0.51: an unobserved confounder would need to explain more than half of the remaining variance of both coupon assignment and spend to reduce the estimate to zero. The deliverable is a Python package with a 49-test suite and CI, a CLI pipeline that runs in about two minutes, a Streamlit dashboard, and a Next.js front end with a CopilotKit copilot whose agent (Claude) answers questions such as "what is the effect for new customers in the north" or "assume region also drives who gets a coupon" by calling the same engine as tools, so every number it states is computed by the pipeline rather than by the model. Validation to date is on synthetic data with known ground truth; applying the pipeline to a real campaign requires the usual causal assumptions (no unobserved confounders, overlap, no interference) to be argued from domain knowledge, and the refutation panel is where that argument is tested.
